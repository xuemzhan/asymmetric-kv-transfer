\begin{abstract}
We investigate what is transferable between language models via KV cache injection. 
Our central thesis is that \textbf{task advantage resides in content streams (value/residual/MLP computation)}, not in addressing geometry (key/attention routing).
Through controlled experiments on Qwen3 model families (8B$\to$0.6B, 4B$\to$1.7B, 4B$\to$0.6B), we demonstrate three key findings:
(1) K-only transfer yields massive improvements (LL: $-6.74$ vs Self $-11.72$, EM: $78.6\%$ vs $42.9\%$), while V-only transfer fails significantly (LL: $-9.97$, EM: $28.6\%$);
(2) V advantage is layer-sparse, concentrated in specific hotspot layers (layers 8, 12) rather than globally distributed;
(3) Raw CCA correlation cannot distinguish K from V (both $\rho \approx 1.0$), yet only K is functionally transferable.
These results support the hypothesis that \textbf{capability lives in weights, not in cache states}---addressing geometry is shared across models, but content computation paths are model-private.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The ability to transfer knowledge between language models via KV cache injection has attracted significant attention.
However, the fundamental question remains: \emph{what exactly is transferable}?
Existing work often treats KV cache as a monolithic entity, transferring both keys and values together.
We decompose this into two orthogonal components:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Addressing geometry (K)}: How the model routes attention to different positions
    \item \textbf{Content streams (V)}: What information flows through the residual stream
\end{itemize}

We hypothesize that addressing geometry is \textbf{cross-model shared}---different models learn similar attention routing patterns for the same task---while content computation is \textbf{model-private}---each model develops its own weight-parameterized computation paths.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Data: Synthetic OOD Domain}

We construct a controlled synthetic domain with:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fictional enterprise entities (military-flavored)
    \item Hidden knowledge graph $K \to$ document rendering $D$
    \item Multi-hop questions (hop-1..4) requiring cross-document reasoning
    \item Entity-cluster splits (train/val/test) preventing leakage
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Mapper Ladder}

We test five mapping strategies with increasing capacity:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{RidgePerHead}: Head-wise ridge regression ($\lambda=1$)
    \item \textbf{CCA}: Canonical Correlation Analysis (rank-128)
    \item \textbf{Procrustes}: Orthogonal Procrustes alignment
    \item \textbf{Whitened}: ZCA whitening + linear mapping
    \item \textbf{Affine}: Full affine transformation (least constrained)
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Four-Way Ablation}

For each mapper, we test four injection modes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Self}: Student generates its own KV (baseline)
    \item \textbf{K-only}: Inject teacher K, student generates V
    \item \textbf{V-only}: Inject teacher V, student generates K
    \item \textbf{Joint}: Inject both teacher K and V
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Answer Log-Likelihood (LL)}: Teacher-forced log-likelihood of answer tokens
    \item \textbf{Exact Match (EM)}: Greedy decoding correctness
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Phase 0: Gate G0 --- Strong Mapper Ablation}

\textbf{Research Question}: Does V transfer fail even with strong mappers?

\textbf{Pre-registered Gate}: V-only injection must not significantly exceed Self baseline (Wilcoxon $p < 0.05$, Cohen $d > 0.8$).

\textbf{Statistical Tests}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item K-only vs Self: Wilcoxon $p < 0.001$, Cohen $d = 4.48$ \textbf{(significant)}

    \item Joint $\approx$ K-only ($\Delta = +0.41$ LL), V adds marginal value
\end{itemize}

\textbf{G0 Verdict}: \textbf{PASS} --- V transfer fails even with strong mappers.

\section{Analysis and Discussion}

\subsection{The Addressing-Content Dichotomy}

Our results reveal a fundamental asymmetry:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{K vs V properties}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Property & K (Addressing) & V (Content) \\
\midrule
Transfer success & \textbf{Yes} (LL: $-6.74$, EM: $78.6\%$) & No (LL: $-9.97$, EM: $28.6\%$) \\
Raw CCA correlation & $\rho \approx 1.0$ & $\rho \approx 1.0$ \\
Layer distribution & Global (all layers) & Layer-sparse (layers 8, 12) \\
Effective rank & High & High ($\approx$ full rank) \\
\midrule
\textbf{Interpretation} & Cross-model shared & Model-private \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Why V Transfer Fails}

Despite high raw correlation ($\rho_V \approx 1.0$), V transfer fails because:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Content computation paths diverge}: Each model develops its own weight-parameterized computation for value streams
    \item \textbf{V advantage is layer-sparse}: Global V injection introduces noise in 26/28 layers, drowning the signal from hotspot layers (8, 12)
    \item \textbf{Capability lives in weights}: The student model cannot use teacher V because its MLP/FFN layers expect different input distributions
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why K Transfer Succeeds}

K transfer succeeds because:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Attention routing is geometry-preserving}: Different models learn similar attention patterns for the same task
    \item \textbf{K advantage is global}: K information is distributed across all layers, so global injection works
    \item \textbf{Shared addressing space}: Keys define a shared geometric structure for attention
\end{enumerate}

\section{Implications}

\subsection{For Knowledge Distillation}

Traditional knowledge distillation transfers \emph{outputs} (logits, soft labels).
Our work suggests transferring \emph{addressing geometry} (K) is more effective than transferring \emph{content representations} (V).

\subsection{For KV Cache Compression}

KV cache compression methods that treat K and V symmetrically may be suboptimal.
Our results suggest:
\begin{itemize}
    \item K compression should be conservative (routing is shared)
    \item V compression can be aggressive (content is model-private)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{For Model Merging}

When merging models, attention routing (K) can be averaged or interpolated more safely than value streams (V).

\section{Limitations and Future Work}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Data scale}: Train=42 samples limits statistical power (though effects are large, $d=4.48$)
    \item \textbf{Model scale}: Only tested on Qwen3 family; need broader model coverage
    \item \textbf{Phase 3/4}: Rate-advantage law and scaling law experiments pending
    \item \textbf{Low-rank hypothesis (P5)}: Not supported by effective rank analysis; may need different formulation
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

We demonstrate that KV cache transfer between language models decomposes into two orthogonal components:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Addressing geometry (K)}: Cross-model shared, globally transferable
    \item \textbf{Content streams (V)}: Model-private, layer-sparse, not transferable
\end{itemize}

This supports the thesis that \textbf{capability is content, not addressing}---task advantage resides in weight-parameterized computation paths, not in cache states.

The implications are fundamental: \emph{capability cannot be transmitted as state, only as weights}.
