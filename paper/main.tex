% ABSTRACT
% ============================================================
\begin{abstract}
Transferring key--value (KV) caches across language models promises to avoid
recomputation when a model is upgraded or an ensemble changes. We revisit this
claim within the Qwen3 family (0.6B--8B; six teacher--student pairs; three
seeds) and report a diagnostic negative result followed by a constructive fix.
First, the standard protocol overstates transfer. Its greedy exact-match metric
is computed on a cache that already contains the gold answer, and its primary
metric---teacher-forced answer log-likelihood---is reproduced by
wrong-document, random, and all-zero caches alike. Under a corrected evaluator,
K-only, V-only, and Joint injection reach at most $0.44$ exact match against
$0.44$--$0.90$ for the student's own document cache: no standard-mapped
teacher state transfers task performance in any of the six pairs. Second, the
failure is consumer-side rather than state-side. The student's attention
routing under mapped teacher keys differs from its own routing (top-1 key
agreement $0.59$--$0.70$), and task performance returns when the \emph{mapper}
is fit in the consumer's space: an attention-output/$W_O$-aware value mapper
raises V-only exact match from $0.11$ to $0.94$ on
$1.7$B$\to0.6$B and from $0.00$ to $0.25$--$0.27$ on
$8$B$\to0.6$B (three seeds). Third, a rank-8 low-rank adaptation of the
student's projections ($\sim$0.7M parameters, trained only on calibration
teacher states) restores K/V/Joint exact match to
$0.94/0.94/0.83$ for $1.7$B$\to0.6$B (three seeds) and
$0.79/0.36/0.47$ for the flagship $8$B$\to0.6$B pair (three seeds), while the
same adapter trained on the student's own states stays far lower
($0.20/0.35/0.18$ and $0.11/0.11/0.12$). Cross-model KV transfer
is a state--consumer compatibility problem, and it is fixable on the consumer
side.
\end{abstract}

% ============================================================
% 1. INTRODUCTION
% ============================================================
\section{Introduction}

KV caches are expensive to rebuild. A single long-context request can hold
gigabytes of cache, and that state is discarded whenever a model is upgraded or
an ensemble is assembled. Reusing it across models would remove the
recomputation, but only if the receiving model can actually \emph{consume} the
transferred state. Recent work maps KV caches across models with linear
translators~\cite{heo2026crossmodel,fu2026c2c,lee2026translators,dery2026latentalign},
and reports that keys can be predicted much more accurately than values. The
natural reading is that addressing geometry (K) is model-agnostic while content
(V) is weight-bound.

This paper argues that this reading rests on two measurement choices that do
not survive scrutiny, and that once they are fixed the picture changes from
``which component transfers'' to ``when can a consumer read a transferred
state.'' Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A corrected evaluation and a control suite.}
    The greedy exact-match (EM) used in the standard protocol is computed on a
    cache that has already been advanced through the query \emph{and the gold
    answer}, so generation starts by reproducing an answer the model has just
    seen. A second defect double-feeds the last query token. After fixing both
    and scoring against a full-prefill reference, no standard-mapped teacher
    state (K-only, V-only, or Joint) transfers task performance on any of six
    Qwen3 pairs: corrected EM is at most $0.44$ (one arm on one pair) against
    $0.44$--$0.90$ for the student's own cache. Teacher-forced
    log-likelihood---which we reproduce exactly from prior work---is not a
    transfer metric: a wrong document, a random Gaussian key with matched
    moments, or an all-zero KV reproduces $92\%$--$114\%$ of the K-only
    log-likelihood gain while yielding corrected EM $\approx 0$.

  \item \textbf{A consumer-side diagnosis.}
    The failure localizes to the receiving model, not to the transferred
    content. Replacing the student's layer map with offsets or a random
    permutation does not reduce transfer, so layer alignment is not the
    controlling variable. The student's attention routing under mapped teacher
    keys diverges from its own routing (top-1 key agreement $0.59$--$0.70$),
    and fitting the value mapper in the consumer's space
    (attention-output / $W_O$) recovers V-only EM from $0.05/0.00$ to
    $0.93/0.27$ on $1.7$B and $8$B students respectively, whereas a
    shuffled-target mapper stays near the floor.

  \item \textbf{A consumption-side fix.}
    A rank-8 low-rank correction on the student's attention projections
    ($\sim$0.7M parameters), trained only on calibration examples with injected
    teacher KV, restores K/V/Joint EM to $0.94/0.94/0.83$
    ($1.7$B$\to0.6$B) and $0.79/0.36/0.47$
    ($8$B$\to0.6$B), each over three seeds. The effect requires training on
    teacher states (a student-state adapter stays near $0.12$) and is strongest
    for content-matched teacher states, though it retains a generic
    teacher-state-distribution component.
\end{enumerate}

The practical message is that cross-model KV transfer is not blocked by a
missing linear map between representation spaces; it is blocked by a mismatch
between the transferred state and the consumer that reads it. That mismatch is
diagnosable (routing divergence) and repairable (consumer-space mappers and a
tiny adapter).

% 2. RELATED WORK

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Cross-Model KV Cache Transfer}
The closest line of work maps KV state across models.
Heo et al.~\cite{heo2026crossmodel} introduce a closed-form per-head ridge
mapper with RoPE-stripped keys and cross-layer source selection for prefill
reuse within a model family, and report that keys are more predictable than
values.
Cache-to-Cache~\cite{fu2026c2c} transfers semantic states between LLMs for
direct communication; Mixture-of-Translators~\cite{lee2026translators}
translates caches across heterogeneous LLMs; LatentAlign~\cite{dery2026latentalign}
aligns latent spaces through KV-cache alignment.
Prior work transfers full KV states with engineered mappings and evaluates with
teacher-forced likelihood or greedy overlap. Our contribution is diagnostic
and constructive: we show that the standard evaluation can report transfer
where none exists functionally, localize the failure to the consumer, and
recover transfer by adapting the consumer rather than the state.

\subsection{KV Cache Optimization}
FlashAttention~\cite{dao2022flashattention} reduces memory traffic;
speculative decoding~\cite{leviathan2023fast,chen2023accelerating} reduces
latency; KV quantization
(KIVI~\cite{kivi2024icml}, KVQuant~\cite{kvquant2024nips}) compresses the
cache. These operate within one model; we study cross-model consumption.

\subsection{Compression, Distillation, Merging, and Stitching}
Quantization~\cite{frantar2023gptq,dettmers2024qlora}, distillation
\cite{hinton2015distilling,touvron2023llama}, model
merging~\cite{ties2023merging,dare2024icml}, and model
stitching~\cite{bansal2021stitching} all modify or combine weights. Our
consumption adapter is closest in spirit to parameter-efficient adaptation: it
adds a small low-rank module while leaving the pretrained model and the state
mapper frozen.

\subsection{Attention and Positional Structure}
The Transformer~\cite{vaswani2017attention} separates keys (addressing) from
values (content); RoPE~\cite{su2024rope} adds positional structure to keys. We
strip positional structure before mapping keys, and treat the consumer's
routing ($A=\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top)$) and output pathway
($O=AVW_O$) as the objects of study.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Setup}
We are given a teacher $T$ and a student $S$ with $L_T$ and $L_S$ layers. For a
document $d$, each model produces a KV cache
$C_m(d)=\{(K_m^\ell,V_m^\ell)\}_{\ell=1}^{L_m}$. A mapper $M_{K},M_{V}$ maps
teacher state to student space, using a layer map
$\pi:[L_S]\to[L_T]$ (proportional for unequal depth, identity for equal depth).
For keys we strip rotary position embeddings before mapping and re-apply the
student's positions afterwards. We then inject the mapped document state into
the student, which sees only the query at generation time, and measure whether
it can answer a held-out question.

\subsection{Data}
We use a synthetic out-of-domain (OOD) corpus: a hidden knowledge graph of
fictional entities is rendered into project-status documents, questions are
sampled with hop depths $1$--$4$, and answers are verified programmatically.
Splits are made by entity cluster (train 70 / val 28 / test 56 per seed), so
test entities never appear in training. Each document is reused across roughly
seven questions, so we additionally report document-clustered
confidence intervals. As a second domain we evaluate on SQuAD with a mapper
calibrated only on the synthetic OOD training split.

\subsection{Injection Protocol}
For each evaluation sample we construct four arms from the document state:
\textbf{Self} (student K and V), \textbf{K-only} (mapped teacher K + student V),
\textbf{V-only} (student K + mapped teacher V), and \textbf{Joint} (mapped
teacher K + mapped teacher V). The student prefills the query against the
injected cache and we score the gold answer.

\subsection{Corrected Evaluation}
The standard implementation advances a single cache through the query and the
gold answer during scoring, and then calls the same (mutated) cache for greedy
generation; the model therefore reproduces an answer it has just been shown.
A second defect re-feeds the final query token as the first generation input.
We use a corrected evaluator: teacher-forced log-likelihood and greedy
generation each start from a \emph{fresh} cache, and the first generated token
is taken from the prefill logits. We verified that this evaluator agrees with a
full-prefill reference implementation (exact-match agreement $6/6$ on a probe
set; only tokenization drift differs).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth]{figures/fig_protocol.pdf}
\caption{Four-arm injection protocol. The student's own document cache is the
reference; K-only and V-only isolate addressing and content; Joint is the full
handoff.}
\label{fig:protocol}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Mappers}
The default mapper is a per-(layer, head) affine ridge map fit on calibration
data. We additionally define two consumer-space mappers for values:
(i) an \textbf{attention-output-aware} mapper that matches
$A_S V_T \to A_S V_S$ on calibration, i.e.\ it is fit where the student reads
value; and (ii) a \textbf{$W_O$-aware} mapper that additionally projects through
the student's output projection and satisfies, per KV head,
$M_h W_{O,q} \approx N_q$ for every grouped query head $q$ in the GQA group.
All mappers are trained on the calibration split only.

\subsection{Consumption Adapter}
To test whether the receiving model can be made compatible, we add a rank-$r$
low-rank correction to the student's attention projections,
$\mathrm{proj}'(x)=Wx+B(Ax)$ with $B$ initialized to zero, and train only
$\{A,B\}$ on the calibration split by next-token cross-entropy with mapped
teacher KV injected. The student and mapper are frozen. Unless stated otherwise
$r=8$ and the adapted module is \texttt{o\_proj}; we also ablate which
projection is adapted.

\subsection{Metrics and Controls}
Our primary task metric is corrected greedy exact match; teacher-forced answer
log-likelihood (LL) is reported as a secondary, clearly non-task metric. We
compare against content controls: a wrong-document cache (a different project's
state), a random Gaussian cache with matched per-layer moments, an all-zero
cache, and a token-shuffled (deranged) cache. For the adapter we additionally
train on student KV and on shuffled-document teacher KV.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The Standard Protocol Does Not Transfer Task Performance}
Table~\ref{tab:main} reports corrected EM and LL for all six pairs (three
seeds). The LL deltas reproduce prior work exactly (e.g.\ $8$B$\to0.6$B
K-only $+2.62$; V-only $-0.23$), confirming that the pipeline itself is
unchanged; what changes is the task metric. Corrected EM for the student's own
cache is $0.44$--$0.90$, but every standard-mapped teacher arm is at most
$0.44$ (V-only on $8$B$\to4$B), and the flagship pair is $0.000$ for all three
arms. Large LL gains ($+8.52$ for $8$B$\to4$B K-only) do not translate into
answers.

\subsection{Teacher-Forced Log-Likelihood Is Content-Agnostic}
The LL gains that a likelihood-only protocol would read as transfer are not
about content. Over three seeds, replacing the teacher's document with another
project's document (a wrong cache) leaves the K-only LL gain within $1\%$
($+2.61$ vs $+2.62$); a random Gaussian key with matched moments recovers
$89\%$ ($+2.33$); an all-zero cache recovers $105\%$ ($+2.75$); Joint behaves
the same ($+4.50$ wrong-document vs $+4.52$ real). Every one of these arms
yields corrected EM $\approx 0$ in every seed, while a token-shuffled
\emph{student} cache (same content, scrambled order) keeps EM at
$0.89\pm0.04$, confirming the evaluator can use context when it is present.
Figure~\ref{fig:controls} summarizes this.

\subsection{Second Domain}
On SQuAD with a mapper calibrated only on the synthetic OOD split, corrected
EM is $0.37$ for Self and $0.00$ for K-only, V-only, and Joint in all three
seeds, while the K-only LL gain is $+5.24$ on average
($p<5\times10^{-7}$). The negative result and the LL/EM dissociation replicate
out of domain.

\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Layer Alignment Is Not the Controlling Variable}
For the equal-layer pair $1.7$B$\to0.6$B we vary only the layer map. Proportional
(identity) mapping gives corrected V-only EM $0.05$--$0.14$ across three seeds;
offsets and a random permutation give $0.00$ in every seed, and the permutation
actually \emph{increases} the V LL delta ($+1.35$ vs $+0.83$ on seed 0). For the
unequal-layer pair $8$B$\to0.6$B, proportional mapping gives the best and most
stable K/Joint LL ($+4.41$--$+4.68$ Joint over three seeds), while offsets,
permutations, and a calibration-learned top-1 source-layer selection reduce it
and are unstable; corrected V-only EM stays at or below $0.11$ for every map and
seed. Layer-map quality therefore does not gate functional transfer.

\subsection{Routing Diverges, and Consumer-Space Mapping Recovers Values}
We compare the student's attention maps under its own keys and under mapped
teacher keys. Mean top-1 document-token agreement is $0.70$ for
$1.7$B$\to0.6$B and $0.59$ for $8$B$\to0.6$B (cosine $0.97$/$0.93$); routing is
perturbed more on the large-gap pair, and values are stable across three seeds
($0.698$--$0.701$ for $1.7$B; $0.577$--$0.590$ for $8$B). Fitting the value
mapper in the consumer's space closes much of the gap
(Table~\ref{tab:mappers}). The attention-output-aware mapper raises V-only EM
to $0.94$ on $1.7$B$\to0.6$B and $0.25$ on $8$B$\to0.6$B (three seeds); the
$W_O$-aware mapper reaches $0.90$ / $0.27$. A mapper fit to the same objective
but with shuffled (wrong-document) targets stays at $0.08$ / $0.07$, showing the
recovery uses document content.

\subsection{A Consumption Adapter Recovers Transfer}
We now adapt the consumer. A rank-8 correction on the student's projections
trained on calibration teacher KV recovers the teacher arms
(Table~\ref{tab:adapter}). For $1.7$B$\to0.6$B (three seeds) K/V/Joint reach
$0.94/0.94/0.83$; for the flagship $8$B$\to0.6$B pair K/V/Joint reach
$0.79/0.36/0.47$ (three seeds) from $0.00$. The same adapter trained on the
student's own states is far weaker ($0.20/0.35/0.18$ and $0.11/0.11/0.12$), so
the recovery requires the teacher-state distribution. A shuffled-content adapter
is intermediate ($0.46$ and $0.13$ Joint), indicating a generic teacher-state
component on top of the content-specific one. Adapting \texttt{o\_proj} and
\texttt{v\_proj} is the most
effective and most specific; adapting \texttt{q\_proj,k\_proj} also helps but
less selectively.

\subsection{Held-out Layer Selection}
Scanning all $28$ student layers on the validation split and freezing the top
two layers ($12,16$; $L_{12}$ ranks first and $L_8$ fourth/fifth in all three
seeds) reproduces the previously reported value hotspot under the corrected
metric: test EM is $0.35$ for the validation-selected pair and
$0.22$ for the post-hoc $8,12$ pair (Self $0.91$). Selective value injection
therefore carries some answer content, but far too little for task transfer.

\subsection{Cost}
For completeness, the byte crossover between transmitting a mapper and
transmitting a KV cache remains at roughly $2$K tokens for fp32 mappers and
fp16 KV; it is a heuristic, ignores quantization, and does not bear on the
state--consumer findings above.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Implications}
The dominant narrative for cross-model KV transfer---keys transfer because
addressing geometry is shared, values do not because content is weight-bound
---is not supported once task performance is measured correctly. Mapped teacher
K and V are linearly predictable (keys more than values) and improve
teacher-forced likelihood, but the receiving model does not turn them into
answers, and the likelihood improvement is reproduced by content-free caches.
What matters is whether the consumer's attention routing and output pathway
can read the transferred state. That condition is diagnosable (routing
divergence) and fixable: fitting value mappers where the student reads value
recovers a large part of V transfer, and a tiny low-rank adaptation of the
consumer recovers most of K/V/Joint transfer. Practically, this suggests that
cross-model cache reuse should budget a small consumer adaptation rather than
trusting a state-space mapper, and that evaluation must include content
controls.

\subsection{Limitations}
Our study is within a single model family (Qwen3, $0.6$B--$8$B) and a single
architecture, so we cannot claim cross-family generality. The synthetic corpus
reuses each document across $\sim$7 questions and shares a template across
documents, which makes ``wrong-document'' controls conservative; we therefore
report document-clustered intervals and interpret the shuffled-content adapter
result cautiously. The adapter is trained on the same task distribution as
evaluation (held-out questions and documents), so it measures repairability of
consumption, not task generalization. The $1.7$B adapter shows high
seed-to-seed variance. We use linear affine mappers as the baseline; nonlinear
mappers remain open. Finally, we report a diagnostic negative result for the
protocol as published, but our corrected numbers use our own evaluator and
reference probes.

\subsection{Future Work}
Three directions follow: (i) a routing-aware key mapper that optimizes the
attention logits directly, to close the residual gap on large-gap pairs;
(ii) cross-family evaluation (Qwen$\to$Llama) to test the boundary of the
consumption adapter; and (iii) systems measurements of mapper and adapter
latency against re-prefill.

\subsection{Conclusion}
Cross-model KV transfer is a state--consumer compatibility problem. Standard
linear maps make teacher state predictable but not usable; a corrected
evaluation and content controls reveal that the reported transfer is largely an
artifact of the metric; and both consumer-space mappers and a small
consumption adapter restore task performance. We hope the diagnostic protocol
and the consumer-side fix become standard tools for cross-model cache reuse.
