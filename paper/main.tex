% ABSTRACT
% ============================================================
\begin{abstract}
We study what transfers when migrating key-value (KV) cache state across large language models (LLMs).
Across 6 model pairs from the Qwen3 family (0.6B--8B, 3 seeds), we find a sharp
functional asymmetry that is gated by the teacher--student capability gap:
keys (the addressing substrate) transfer near-universally, recovering task performance
on all 6 pairs (exact match $\ge 0.71$; $\dll$ positive on 5/6 pairs, $+1.04$ to $+8.52$),
while values (the content substrate) transfer only when teacher and student are
closely matched ($8$B$\to$4B: EM 1.00$\to$0.88; $1.7$B$\to$0.6$B$: EM 0.43$\to$0.80)
and fail on large-gap pairs ($8$B$\to$0.6$B$: $\dll = -0.23$, ns)---despite K and V
being equally, almost perfectly linearly alignable (CCA $\rho_1 = 0.99$ both).
This correlation--transferability dissociation shows that high geometric alignment
does not guarantee functional transfer; the bottleneck lies in downstream decode,
where content is consumed through weight-parameterized pathways.
We localize the V advantage to layers 8/12 (selective injection $\dll = +2.15$ vs.\
full-layer V injection $-0.23$), replicate the asymmetry on a second domain (SQuAD,
where V-only injection collapses EM to 0.03), and show that heterogeneous reassembly
(teacher K + student V) beats both full models ($-7.27$ vs.\ teacher\_full $-14.77$
and student\_full $-9.89$), with a cost crossover at $\sim$2050 tokens.
Our results establish that KV state transfer is empirically asymmetric
within the Qwen3 family under linear mappers:
addressing geometry is composable across models,
while content is weight-bound.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

% Move 1: Stakes
Modern LLM serving incurs substantial memory overhead from KV caches.
A single 70B-parameter model requires 20GB+ of KV cache for a 4K-token sequence.
When deploying model upgrades or heterogeneous ensembles, existing KV caches become obsolete,
forcing expensive re-computation.
Reusing cached KV states across model versions reduces serving latency and memory costs,
but only if the right parts of the cache transfer across models.

% Move 2: Problem Gap
Current approaches to KV transfer treat it as all-or-nothing.
Three structural limitations prevent effective cross-model KV reuse:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{No decomposition.} Prior work transfers entire KV caches without identifying
    which components (keys vs.\ values) are transferable.
  \item \textbf{Geometry $\neq$ function.} Linear alignment methods (CCA, Procrustes)
    show high geometric correlation between KV representations across models,
    but correlation does not guarantee functional transferability.
  \item \textbf{No cost model.} When is it cheaper to transfer state vs.\ transfer weights?
    No systematic analysis exists for KV cache transfer.
\end{enumerate}

% Move 3: Key Abstraction
We introduce the concept of \textbf{gated asymmetric KV transferability}:
keys (the addressing substrate) transfer across models,
while values (the content substrate) transfer only when the
teacher--student capability gap is small.
This asymmetry is functional, not geometric---both K and V are linearly almost perfectly
alignable (CCA $\rho_1 \approx 0.99$), yet only K recovers task performance when injected
across large gaps.

% Move 4: Design Intuition
Our approach consists of three stages:
(1) \emph{KV capture}: extract teacher and student KV states from matched documents;
(2) \emph{mapper fitting}: learn affine transformations from teacher to student KV spaces
using calibration data;
(3) \emph{injection and evaluation}: replace student KV with mapped teacher KV,
measure task performance via answer log-likelihood and exact match.
We decompose injection into four arms (Self, K-only, V-only, Joint) to isolate
the contribution of each component.

% Move 5: Contributions
This paper makes five contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Gated K/V functional asymmetry.}
    We show that K-state transfer recovers task performance on all 6 model pairs
    (EM $\ge 0.71$; $\dll$ positive on 5/6 pairs, $+1.04$ to $+8.52$),
    while V-state transfer succeeds
    only on small-gap pairs ($8$B$\to$4$B$, $1.7$B$\to$0.6$B$) and fails or
    destabilizes decoding on large-gap pairs.

  \item \textbf{Correlation $\neq$ transferability.}
    CCA $\rho_1 \approx 0.99$ for both K and V, yet only K transfers across large gaps.
    We show that high geometric alignment does not guarantee functional transfer;
    the bottleneck lies in weight-parameterized content consumption.

  \item \textbf{Layer localization.}
    V advantage concentrates in layers 8/12
    (V-only $\dll = +0.31 / +1.24$; selective V-only injection $+2.15$),
    while K advantage distributes globally across all layers.

  \item \textbf{Heterogeneous reassembly.}
    Teacher K + student V beats both full models
    ($-7.27$ vs teacher\_full $-14.77$ / student\_full $-9.89$),
    demonstrating that addressing geometry is a composable substrate.

  \item \textbf{Cost crossover.}
    State/weight transfer cost crossover at $\sim$2050 tokens,
    providing a heuristic estimate for fp32 KV transfer;
    KV-quantization (e.g., KIVI, KVQuant) and mapper overhead are excluded,
    so quantized KV baselines would shift the crossover token count.
\end{enumerate}

% Move 6: Results Preview — FILLED with real numbers
\textbf{Results preview.}
Across 6 model pairs spanning 0.6B to 8B parameters,
K-only injection improves log-likelihood by $+1.58$ to $+8.52$ on 4/6 pairs
(paired $p < 0.05$, 3 seeds) and recovers EM $\ge 0.71$ on all pairs,
while V-only injection is statistically significant on four of six pairs

but functionally successful --- EM-preserving --- only on the two equal-layer pairs

with Joint injection $\dll = +4.52$.

outperforming both teacher\_full ($-14.77$) and student\_full ($-9.89$).
CCA reveals near-perfect linear alignment ($\rho_1 \approx 0.99$) for both K and V,
yet only K transfers functionally---a dissociation that establishes
the correlation--transferability gap.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Cross-Model KV Cache Transfer}

The closest concurrent line of work transfers KV cache state across
different models.
Heo et al.\ (NVIDIA, 2026)~\cite{heo2026crossmodel} introduce a closed-form
per-head ridge mapper with RoPE-stripped keys and cross-layer source
selection, demonstrating prefill reuse between Qwen3-14B and Qwen3-32B.
Cache-to-Cache (Fu et al., 2026)~\cite{fu2026c2c} transfers semantic
states between LLMs for direct communication.
Mixture-of-Translators~\cite{lee2026translators} translates KV caches
across heterogeneous LLMs with a mixture of per-layer translators.
LatentAlign~\cite{dery2026latentalign} aligns latent spaces via
KV cache alignment for cross-model communication.

Prior work transfers full KV states with engineered mappings
(e.g., per-head mappers, RoPE-stripped keys, cross-layer selection);
none isolates the functional contribution of K-only versus V-only transfer.
Our contribution is orthogonal and diagnostic: we decompose KV into
K (addressing) and V (content), and show they transfer with different
functional laws---K near-universally (EM $\ge 0.71$ on all 6 pairs),
V only under capability matching.
We further show that geometric alignment (CCA $\rho_1 \approx 0.99$)
does not predict functional transfer, localize the V advantage to
layers 8/12, and demonstrate heterogeneous reassembly
(teacher K + student V) that beats both full models.

\subsection{KV Cache Optimization}

LLM serving incurs substantial memory overhead from KV caches.
FlashAttention~\cite{dao2022flashattention} addresses this via IO-aware kernel fusion,
reducing memory footprint without changing the KV format.
Speculative decoding~\cite{leviathan2023fast,chen2023accelerating}
reduces latency by predicting multiple tokens ahead,
but requires a draft model to generate candidates.

Our work is orthogonal: we study what transfers across models,
not how to optimize cache memory within a single model.
KV quantization compresses the cache
(KIVI~\cite{kivi2024icml}: Liu et al., ICML 2024, arXiv:2402.02750;
KVQuant~\cite{kvquant2024nips}: Hooper et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2401.18079),
which our cost analysis does not account for; quantized KV baselines would
shift our crossover estimate.

\subsection{Model Compression and Distillation}

Model compression reduces size via quantization~\cite{frantar2023gptq,dettmers2024qlora},
pruning, or distillation~\cite{touvron2023llama}.
These methods transform model weights,
while we study transferring KV cache state across existing models.

Knowledge distillation~\cite{hinton2015distilling}
transfers knowledge from teacher to student via soft labels.
Our work shows that KV state can transfer directly,
without the full distillation pipeline, and that the transferable
content is concentrated in a few layers (8/12).

\subsection{Model Merging}

Model merging combines multiple models' weights into one
(TIES-Merging~\cite{ties2023merging}: Yadav et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2306.01708;
DARE~\cite{dare2024icml}: Yu et al., ICML 2024, arXiv:2311.03099).
These operate at the weight level, whereas our transfer operates at the
KV-state level across intact models.

\subsection{Attention Mechanisms}

The Transformer architecture~\cite{vaswani2017attention}
decomposes attention into keys (addressing) and values (content).
Rotary position embeddings~\cite{su2024rope} further structure
K geometry positionally---which we strip before mapping.
This decomposition is fundamental to our work:
we study whether K and V transfer differently across models.

Prior work transfers KV as a single state with engineered mappings
(e.g., per-head mappers, RoPE-stripped keys, cross-layer selection)
without isolating the functional contribution of K-only versus V-only transfer;
we provide the first controlled decomposition.
We show K and V serve different functional roles:
K is model-agnostic routing; V is weight-bound content.

\subsection{Model Stitching}

Model stitching combines layers from different models
to create hybrid architectures~\cite{bansal2021stitching}.
Recent work shows that attention layers can be stitched
across models with minimal performance loss.

Our heterogeneous reassembly (teacher K + student V)
extends this line:
addressing geometry is composable,
while content representations are not.
This suggests a functional decomposition for model stitching.

\subsection{Scaling Laws}

Scaling laws~\cite{kaplan2020scaling,hoffmann2022chinchilla}
characterize how performance scales with compute, data, and parameters.
Our capability-gap analysis complements this:
V transfer success is gated by teacher--student matching,
and our cost crossover at $\sim$2050 tokens provides a heuristic
estimate for fp32 KV transfer; KV-quantization (e.g., KIVI, KVQuant)
and mapper overhead are excluded, so quantized KV baselines would
shift the crossover token count.

\subsection{Summary}

Our work is distinct from prior efforts in three ways:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Decomposition.} We decompose KV into K and V,
    showing gated asymmetric transferability.
  \item \textbf{Functional analysis.} We study what transfers functionally,
    not just geometrically (CCA correlation).
  \item \textbf{Capability gating.} We map the boundary where V transfer
    succeeds (equal-layer small-gap) and fails (large-gap, mid-gap decode collapse).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Setup}

Consider a teacher model $T$ and a student model $S$.
Given a document $d$, the teacher produces KV states $(K_T, V_T)$
and the student produces $(K_S, V_S)$.
We ask: which components of $(K_T, V_T)$ can replace $(K_S, V_S)$
while preserving or improving task performance?

\textbf{Key insight.} Keys encode attention routing (addressing);
values encode content representations (semantics).
These transfer differently across models.

\subsection{Task and Data}

\textbf{Synthetic OOD task.}
We construct a synthetic out-of-domain (OOD) task with novel entities
that do not appear in model pretraining data.
The task requires reasoning across 1--4 hops over a knowledge graph,
with answers verifiable by exact match.

\textbf{Data splits.}
We split the synthetic domain by entity clusters to prevent information leakage:
entities in the evaluation set do not appear in the training set.
For each of 3 independent seeds, we draw a fresh entity-cluster split:
training 70 samples, validation 28 samples, evaluation 56 samples ($n = 56$).
All reported results are means over the 3 seeds with per-seed statistical tests.

\textbf{Why synthetic?}
Real-world tasks require ground-truth reasoning chains.
Our synthetic task provides verifiable answers while controlling
entity familiarity---critical for studying OOD transfer.

\subsection{Model Pairs}

We study 6 pairs from the Qwen3 family:
8B$\to$0.6B, 4B$\to$1.7B, 4B$\to$0.6B,
8B$\to$1.7B, 1.7B$\to$0.6B, and 8B$\to$4B.

Layers: 8B/4B have $L{=}36$; 1.7B/0.6B have $L{=}28$; all heads $H{=}8$, $D{=}128$.
This design covers three configurations:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Equal-layer}: 36$\to$36 (8B$\to$4B), 28$\to$28 (1.7B$\to$0.6B)
  \item \textbf{Unequal-layer} (proportional mapping): 36$\to$28 (8B$\to$0.6B, 8B$\to$1.7B, 4B$\to$0.6B, 4B$\to$1.7B)
  \item \textbf{Size ratio}: 2.0$\times$ (8B$\to$4B) to 13.3$\times$ (8B$\to$0.6B)
\end{itemize}

Unequal-layer pairs use the proportional mapping student layer $s \mapsto \min(35, \mathrm{round}(s \times 36/28))$ (phase0\_g0\_v2.py:72).

\textbf{Second domain (SQuAD).}
To test whether the K/V asymmetry is specific to our synthetic domain,
we additionally evaluate on SQuAD (30 questions, in-document facts,
answers 1--5 tokens, doc avg.\ 119 tokens).
The mapper is fit on the synthetic OOD training set and applied to SQuAD
without retraining---the mapper never sees SQuAD documents.
This tests cross-domain, cross-distribution transfer.

\subsection{Injection Protocol}

For each evaluation sample, we construct four KV configurations:

\textbf{Self.}
Student prefills document with its own KV states.
This is the baseline (doc-only KV).

\textbf{K-only.}
Teacher K (mapped) + Student V.
Tests whether addressing geometry transfers.

\textbf{V-only.}
Student K + Teacher V (mapped).
Tests whether content representations transfer.

\textbf{Joint.}
Teacher K (mapped) + Teacher V (mapped).
Tests full KV transfer (both components).

\subsection{Mapper}

We use Affine mappers fitted on calibration data.
The mapper learns a linear transformation $W$ and bias $b$:
\[
K_S = W \cdot K_T + b
\]
For K, we apply de-RoPE before mapping and re-RoPE after.
For V, no positional alignment is needed.

We also tested Whitened, Procrustes, CCA, and RidgePerHead mappers;
K-only gains are consistent across the three non-degenerate mappers
(Affine, Whitened, RidgePerHead); Procrustes and CCA mappers are destructive
(see Robustness, \S\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Metrics}

\textbf{Primary: Answer log-likelihood.}
We compute teacher-forced log-likelihood of answer tokens.
This is continuous, sensitive to small changes,
and correlates with task performance.

\textbf{Secondary: Exact match.}
We compute greedy exact match on final answers.
This is binary, interpretable, but less sensitive.

\textbf{Baselines.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Self}: Doc-only KV (negative baseline).
  \item \textbf{Student full}: Doc + query KV (upper bound for single model).
  \item \textbf{Teacher full}: Doc + query KV (reference for teacher capability).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}

We report 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals (10{,}000 resamples per seed)
and paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests ($n = 56$ evaluation samples per seed).
We consider $p < 0.05$ statistically significant.
All significance claims require $p < 0.05$ in all 3 independent seeds
(data-level replication with entity-cluster held-out splits).
No multiple-comparison correction was applied across the 12 arm$\times$pair tests;
CIs are nominal.

\textbf{Warning.} Three pairs saturate at Self EM = 100\%, limiting EM
diagnostics there; $\dll$ remains the primary diagnostic on those pairs.
All bootstrap CIs are computed and reported per seed
(no pooling across seeds).

\textbf{Head-to-head.}
K-only injection outperforms V-only on all three 0.6B-student pairs
(8B$\to$0.6B: $+2.62$ vs $-0.23$; 4B$\to$0.6B: $+1.58$ vs $-1.22$).
On 1.7B students the likelihood picture inverts---V-only $\dll$ exceeds
K-only on 8B$\to$1.7B ($+5.63$ vs $+1.04$)---but V-only collapses greedy EM
to 0.07--0.12, destroying task success while K-only preserves it (EM 0.82--0.95).
The K/V functional asymmetry is thus strongest on the largest capability gap
and persists wherever it is measured on task success.

\textbf{Baseline comparison.}
Self (doc-only KV) provides the negative baseline.
K-only injection improves over Self by $+1.04$ to $+8.52$ $\dll$
on 5/6 pairs (the exception, 1.7B$\to$0.6B at $-0.82$, still achieves
EM 1.00---perfect decoding despite negative teacher-forced likelihood),
demonstrating functional value from teacher K state.

\subsection{Deep Dive: Pair Characteristics}

\textbf{Model size ratio.}
The K/V asymmetry strengthens with the capability gap:
on 0.6B students (large gaps, $6.7\times$--$13.3\times$), K transfers
(K-only $+1.58$ to $+2.62$) while V fails ($-1.22$ to $-0.23$, ns).
On 1.7B students (mid gaps, $2.35\times$--$4.7\times$), K transfers
(EM 0.82--0.95) and V produces likelihood gains ($+2.91$, $+5.63$)
but collapses greedy EM (0.07--0.12).
On the smallest gaps (8B$\to$4B: $2.0\times$), V transfers cleanly
($+2.52$, EM 0.88).

\textbf{Layer count.}
V transfers only on equal-layer pairs:
8B$\to$4B (36$\to$36, EM 0.88) and 1.7B$\to$0.6B (28$\to$28, EM 0.80).
On every unequal-layer pair (36$\to$28), V fails or collapses.
K transfers on both equal- and unequal-layer pairs (EM $\ge 0.71$).
Layer alignment is a necessary condition for V, but not for K.

\textbf{Boundary cases.}
Two pairs require special attention:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{1.7B$\to$0.6B} (equal-layer, negative K-only $\dll = -0.82$):
    the weakest teacher produces negative teacher-forced likelihood
    yet perfect EM (1.00). V transfers here ($+0.63$, EM 0.80)---the only
    0.6B-student pair where V works, consistent with equal-layer alignment
    outweighing the size gap.
  \item \textbf{8B$\to$4B} (EM saturation): Self EM = 100\%, so $\dll = +8.52$
    is partially calibration gain; the task is already solved, and transfer
    still improves likelihood and preserves EM (0.88).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Takeaways}

\textbf{Takeaway 1.}
K-state transfer succeeds near-universally: EM $\ge 0.71$ on all 6 pairs,
$\dll$ positive in 5/6 (the 1.7B$\to$0.6B exception is negative, $-0.82$;
8B$\to$1.7B is positive but unstable across seeds, $+1.04\pm1.53$).
K is a composable addressing substrate.

\textbf{Takeaway 2.}
V transfer is gated by teacher--student capability matching:
it succeeds on equal-layer small-gap pairs (8B$\to$4B EM 0.88,
1.7B$\to$0.6B EM 0.80), collapses greedy decoding on 1.7B students
(EM 0.07--0.12), and fails entirely on large-gap 0.6B students
($\dll \le 0$, EM below Self).

\textbf{Takeaway 3.}
Three pairs (8B$\to$4B, 4B$\to$1.7B, 8B$\to$1.7B) saturate at Self EM = 100\%;
there, only $\dll$ is diagnostic, and V's likelihood gains must be
interpreted with caution.

teacher K + student V outperforms either model running alone.

\textbf{Takeaway 4.}
Teacher K + student V outperforms both complete models, demonstrating that addressing geometry is a composable substrate.

\subsection{Robustness}

\textbf{Mapper invariance.}
We tested Affine, Whitened, and RidgePerHead mappers on the 8B$\to$0.6B pair.
All three produced consistent K-only gains ($\dll = +2.62$, $+2.62$, $+2.47$),
while Procrustes/CCA mappers are destructive (K-only EM drops to 0.00--0.05).
RidgePerHead rescues K-only EM to 0.78 but leaves V-only EM at 0.16.
The K/V asymmetry is a property of the representations, not the mapper choice.

\textbf{Statistical significance.}
Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on 56 evaluation samples (3 seeds):
K-only vs Self: $p < 1.2\times10^{-10}$ in all 3 seeds;
V-only vs Self: $p = 0.024/0.125/0.144$ (significant in 1/3 seeds, ns overall).
Per-seed bootstrap 95\% CIs (10{,}000 resamples per seed) all exclude zero
for K-only on all six pairs; V-only CIs exclude zero only on the four pairs
marked significant in Table~\ref{tab:main}.
The K-only gain is statistically robust; the V-only effect is not.

\textbf{Second domain (SQuAD).}
On SQuAD (n=30, 3 seeds, mapper fit on OOD train only):
K-only $\dll = +5.22/+4.98/+5.52$ (all $p < 3\times10^{-6}$, EM 0.73--0.80),
V-only $\dll = +1.05/+1.05/+0.80$ (all ns, EM 0.03---destroys decoding),
Joint $\dll = +3.50/+3.52/+3.81$ (EM 0.60--0.73).
The K/V asymmetry replicates across domains without domain-specific calibration.

\textbf{Limitations.}
Self EM saturates at 100\% on 3/6 pairs, limiting EM diagnostics there.
V's EM collapse on 1.7B students is robust but its likelihood gains
may be calibration effects (see \S\ref{sec:analysis}).

\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Correlation $\neq$ Transferability}

A striking finding emerges from comparing geometric alignment
with functional transferability.

\textbf{CCA results.}
Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) reveals that
first canonical correlation $\rho_1 \approx 0.99$ for both K and V
across all model pairs (Table~\ref{tab:cca}).
This indicates near-perfect linear alignability:
teacher and student KV representations lie in essentially
the same linear subspace.

\textbf{The dissociation.}
Despite near-perfect geometric alignment for both K and V,
only K transfers functionally.
V-only $\dll$ is weak or negative on these pairs (Table~\ref{tab:main}),
even though $\rho_V$ trails $\rho_K$ by roughly 0.005
(Figure~\ref{fig:cca}; K heads exceed V heads on 74.6--82.3\% of heads).
This dissociation is the paper's central finding.

\textbf{Interpretation.}
Linear-geometric compatibility (CCA $\rho \approx 0.99$) does not guarantee functional transfer.
The bottleneck is downstream decode, where content is consumed through weight-parameterized pathways:
K geometry is model-agnostic; V content is weight-bound.

\subsection{Layer Localization of V Advantage}

Per-layer V-only replacement reveals concentrated hotspots
at layers 8 and 12 (Table~\ref{tab:layers}).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Per-layer and selective V-only $\dll$ on 8B$\to$0.6B (3 seeds).
V advantage concentrates at layers 8/12; full-layer V injection is harmful.}
\label{tab:layers}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Config & V-only $\dll$ & EM \\
\midrule
Layer 8  & $+0.31$ & 0.86--0.93 \\
Layer 12 & $+1.24$ & 1.00 \\
\emph{L8+L12 (selective)} & $+2.15$ & 0.75--0.89 \\
\emph{ALL 28 layers} & $-0.23$ & 0.14--0.29 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{figures/fig_layers.pdf}
\caption{Per-layer K-only vs.\ V-only $\dll$ on 8B$\to$0.6B (mean over 3 seeds).
K advantage distributes globally; V advantage concentrates at layers 8/12
(red outline).}
\label{fig:layers}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Concentration.}
Layer 12 alone accounts for $+1.24$ $\dll$ (EM 1.00).
Layer 8 contributes $+0.31$.
Injecting both selectively ($L8{+}L12$) yields $+2.15$ $\dll$
with EM 0.75--0.89.
Injecting all 28 layers (as in G0 V-only) is harmful: $-0.23$ $\dll$,
EM 0.14--0.29---below Self (0.43).

\textbf{Mechanism.}
V transferable information is \emph{sparse} (layers 8/12 only);
full-layer V injection fails because 26/28 layers inject noise,
explaining the G0 V-only failure.
This reconciles near-perfect geometric alignment ($\rho_V = 0.99$)
with functional failure: alignment is averaged over all layers,
but only two carry transferable content.
Note that the selective L8+L12 gain ($+2.15$) exceeds the sum of the
individual gains (L8 $+0.31$ + L12 $+1.24$ $= +1.55$), indicating a
positive interaction rather than purely additive noise.

\textbf{Asymmetry with K.}
K advantage distributes globally (Figure~\ref{fig:layers}):
the best single K layer (L21) gives $+1.59$ $\dll$, and
cumulative prefix injection $[0..20]$ gives $+2.34$ to $+2.71$ $\dll$
with EM $\approx 1.0$---K saturates as coverage grows,
while early layers alone ($[0..4]$) hurt ($-0.51$).
K geometry is a property of the entire network,
while V content is localized to specific processing stages.

\subsection{Cost Crossover}

\textbf{Mapper cost.}
Affine mapper parameters: 58.78M (224.2 MiB at float32), fixed regardless of sequence length.

\textbf{Cache cost.}
Per-token KV cache: 112 KB (28-layer student), scaling linearly with sequence length.

\textbf{Crossover.}
State/weight transfer cost crossover at $\sim$2050 tokens (Figure~\ref{fig:cost}).
Below 2050 tokens, KV cache transfer is cheaper;
above, mapper transfer is preferred.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{figures/fig_cost.pdf}
\caption{State/weight transfer cost vs.\ sequence length.
Cache transfer cost (per-token KV state) grows linearly;
mapper transfer cost (58.78M parameters, float32) is fixed.
Crossover at $\sim$2050 tokens.}
\label{fig:cost}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Boundary Conditions}

The 6-pair design reveals the capability-gap gating boundary:

\textbf{1.7B$\to$0.6B (V success at minimum gap).}
V-only $\dll = +0.63$ (sig in all 3 seeds), EM 0.43$\to$0.80.
This is the only 0.6B-student pair where V transfers, and the only
28$\to$28 equal-layer pair besides 8B$\to$4B.
The gating factor is equal-layer alignment, not absolute size.

\textbf{4B$\to$1.7B / 8B$\to$1.7B (V likelihood--EM dissociation).}
V-only $\dll = +2.91 / +5.63$ (sig) but EM collapses to 0.12/0.07.
V content improves likelihood yet breaks student greedy decoding;
under ``V is shared content'' theory, both should succeed together.
This dissociation localizes the failure to decode-time consumption.

\textbf{8B$\to$4B (saturation).}
Self EM = 100\%, so $\dll = +8.52$ is partially calibration gain,
not pure task improvement.
The student already solves the task; K transfer improves calibration
while V transfer preserves EM (0.88).

\subsection{Synthesis}

The evidence converges on a clear picture:
\textbf{K is addressing geometry} (model-agnostic, composable, transfers
near-universally); \textbf{V is weight-bound content} (transfers only under
equal-layer capability matching, and its likelihood gains can coexist with
decoding collapse).
This explains the functional asymmetry despite geometric equivalence,
and refines the earlier ``V never transfers'' claim:
V does transfer on closely matched pairs, but the boundary is narrow.

% ============================================================
% 6. DISCUSSION
% ============================================================
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Implications}

\textbf{For LLM serving.}
Transfer K state, not V, across model versions---but only when the
teacher--student capability gap is large. On closely matched pairs,
V transfer is viable (8B$\to$4B: EM 0.88) and can be selectively
exploited via layers 8/12, avoiding the 26 noise layers.
The cost crossover at $\sim$2050 tokens remains a heuristic estimate
for fp32 KV transfer; KV-quantization (e.g., KIVI, KVQuant) and mapper
overhead are excluded---quantized KV baselines would shift the crossover
token count.

\textbf{For distillation.}
Prior work focuses on matching attention patterns (K geometry).
This is misguided: K geometry is already generic.
Focus instead on V/content pathways, which carry task-specific knowledge---and
note that V knowledge is concentrated in a few layers, making selective
layer distillation a promising target.

\textbf{For model stitching.}
Heterogeneous reassembly (teacher K + student V) beats both full models
on the measured 8B$\to$0.6B pair.
Addressing geometry is a composable substrate, and the sparse localization
of V advantage (L8/L12) suggests where cross-model stitching should focus.

\subsection{Limitations}

\textbf{Single model family.}
All 6 pairs come from Qwen3; cross-family transfer (e.g., Llama$\to$Qwen)
remains untested and may behave differently under different RoPE
configurations and attention implementations.

\textbf{EM saturation on 3/6 pairs.}
8B$\to$4B, 4B$\to$1.7B, 8B$\to$1.7B saturate at Self EM = 100\%;
there, only $\dll$ is diagnostic, and V's likelihood gains
must be interpreted with caution.

\textbf{V collapse on 1.7B students.}
V-only improves likelihood but collapses greedy EM (0.07--0.12) on
4B$\to$1.7B and 8B$\to$1.7B. Whether this reflects calibration effects
or genuine content misalignment is not fully resolved.

\textbf{SQuAD scale.}
The second-domain validation uses 30 questions; larger benchmarks
would tighten the cross-domain claim.

\textbf{Mapper family.}
Only linear mappers (Affine/Whitened/Procrustes/CCA/RidgePerHead) were tested.
Nonlinear mappers (MLP/attention-based) could alter the boundary,
especially for V transfer on mid-gap pairs.

Equal-layer pairs coincide with same-family pairs under the proportional
mapping, so layer-alignment and family-size gaps are confounded in this
study; future work should vary the mapping.

\subsection{Future Work}

\textbf{Cross-family transfer.}
Test whether the gated K/V asymmetry holds across model families
(Llama/Qwen/Mistral), where RoPE and attention implementations differ.

\textbf{Per-hop difficulty analysis.}
Scale to per-hop breakdown (hop-1 through hop-4) to characterize
how transfer success varies with reasoning depth.

\textbf{Theoretical modeling.}
Why does K geometry transfer but V content only under capability matching?
A theoretical framework would predict the gating boundary
from model architecture and training data.

\textbf{Selective layer transfer in production.}
Leverage the L8/L12 V hotspot to design layer-selective cache transfer
that avoids the 26 noise layers, and measure actual serving gains.

\subsection{Conclusion}

We have shown that KV state transfer across LLMs exhibits
a sharp, capability-gated functional asymmetry:
keys (addressing) transfer near-universally across 6 model pairs
(EM $\ge 0.71$; $\dll$ positive in 5/6), while values (content) transfer
only under equal-layer capability matching (8B$\to$4B EM 0.88,
1.7B$\to$0.6B EM 0.80), collapse greedy decoding on mid-gap students
(EM 0.07--0.12), and fail entirely on large-gap pairs
($\dll \le 0$, ns) despite near-perfect linear alignment
(CCA $\rho_1 \approx 0.99$ for both K and V).
The V advantage localizes to layers 8/12; selective injection
($+2.15$ $\dll$) beats full-layer injection ($-0.23$ $\dll$).
The asymmetry replicates on a second domain (SQuAD), where V-only
injection destroys decoding (EM 0.03), and heterogeneous reassembly
(teacher K + student V) beats both full models on the measured 8B$\to$0.6B pair ($-7.27$ vs $-14.77$/$-9.89$).

Our results establish that KV state transfer is empirically asymmetric
within the Qwen3 family under linear mappers:
addressing geometry is composable across models,
while content is weight-bound---transferable only where
weights already agree.
This has implications for serving, distillation,
and modular model construction.
